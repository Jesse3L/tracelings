The best Christmas activities for preschoolers feel like holiday fun but quietly practice a skill: counting ornaments, writing a name on gift tags, snipping wrapping paper, or tracing short holiday words. Most take 10 to 15 minutes and use things already in the house in December. Pick one or two a day, and your child gets steady practice without a single worksheet battle.

Below, the activities are sorted by the skill they build. Each one works at a kitchen table or in a preschool classroom, and there's a simple week plan at the end.

## How to choose Christmas activities for preschoolers

Start with what your child enjoys and what they are working on. A child who loves to move will do better with a counting game than a quiet tracing page. A child who has just started tracing letters may be thrilled to see their name on a real gift.

Keep the bar low. December is busy and loud, and short activities stick better than long ones. If something falls flat, put it away and bring it back next week.

If you're looking for keepsakes to hang on the tree, our [Christmas crafts for preschoolers](/learn/christmas-crafts-for-preschoolers/) has six with step-by-step instructions. The activities here are lighter. They're about practice, not finished decorations.

## Counting activities with ornaments and gifts

Counting real objects helps children connect a number word to an amount. The holidays hand you plenty of things to count.

- **Ornament counting tree.** Draw a large tree on a sheet of poster board and write a number from 1 to 10 at the top. Your child sticks on that many circle stickers or pom poms as ornaments. Change the number each day.
- **Unbreakable ornament sort.** Pour a handful of plastic ornaments into a basket. Your child sorts them by color, then counts each pile. Ask which pile has more.
- **Gift box stacking.** Collect empty boxes in different sizes. Your child stacks them as high as they can and counts the boxes before the tower falls.
- **Bell count and clap.** Ring a jingle bell a few times while your child listens. They hold up that many fingers or clap back the same number.
- **Number tracing in the countdown.** Once counting feels easy, add writing. A [number tracing worksheet for 1 to 20](/number-tracing/1-20/) pairs nicely with a daily countdown, since your child can trace the date's number each morning.

Many 3 year olds count small groups of a few objects, and many 4 and 5 year olds count higher, sometimes past 10. It's very common to skip a number or count one item twice. Keep it playful and count together.

## Gift tag name writing

A name is often the first word preschoolers want to write, and gift tags give that word a real job. Kids tend to try harder when they know someone will read it.

1. Cut plain cardstock into rectangles about 2 by 3 inches, or use blank store tags.
2. Write "From" on each tag, then your child's name in dotted letters below it.
3. Your child traces their name with a marker or crayon.
4. Punch a hole and tie each tag onto a gift with ribbon or yarn.

To save time, print a [free name tracing worksheet](/name-tracing/) and cut it into strips. Each strip is one tag. A child who can trace their name well can try writing it once without dots on the back of the tag.

For a 3 year old, trace only the first letter and let them decorate the rest with a sticker. For a 5 year old, add "To" and the name of the person who gets the gift.

## Wrapping paper cutting for scissor practice

Leftover wrapping paper is perfect for scissor practice. It's thin, colorful, and nobody minds if it gets chopped up.

- **Fringe snipping.** Cut wrapping paper into strips about 2 inches wide. Your child makes small snips along one edge to make fringe. Single snips are the first scissor skill many children learn.
- **Cut along the lines.** Many wrapping papers have stripes or a grid on the back. Your child cuts along one line from edge to edge.
- **Confetti jar.** Your child cuts strips into small squares and drops them into a clear jar. Use the confetti later for gluing.

Use child safety scissors and sit with your child the whole time. Show them how to hold the paper with the helper hand and keep the thumb on top of the scissors. Some kids aren't ready for scissors at 3. Tearing paper builds the same hand strength in the meantime.

## Tracing a short holiday word list

Short holiday words make tracing feel like part of the celebration. Choose four to six words and keep them simple:

- star
- tree
- gift
- bell
- snow
- joy

Type your list into our [word tracing worksheet maker](/word-tracing/) to print dotted letters on handwriting lines. Your child traces one or two words a day, not the whole list at once.

Make it hands-on. Hide picture cards for each word around the room. When your child finds the star card, they trace "star." Pre-readers can match the word to the picture, while early readers can try reading it aloud first.

## Calm activities for busy December days

Some days have no room for anything new. Keep a few low-energy options ready:

- **Holiday coloring.** A stack of [Christmas coloring pages](/coloring-pages/christmas-coloring-pages/) is great for waiting rooms, travel, or the hour before guests arrive.
- **Cookie dough letters.** Roll store-bought dough into snakes and shape the first letter of your child's name before baking.
- **Light spotting walk.** After dark, walk or drive past houses with lights. Count the lit trees or name the colors you see.
- **Read and retell.** Read a favorite holiday picture book, then have your child tell you what happened first, next, and last.

## What to expect at 3, 4 and 5

Children grow at their own pace, so treat these as loose ranges:

- **Many 3 year olds** are working on holding a crayon, making lines and circles, and counting a few objects.
- **Many 4 year olds** can trace their name and count to 10 or so, with letters that may be large or backward.
- **Many 5 year olds** write their first name without a model and enjoy tracing and reading short words.

Backward letters, skipped numbers, and wobbly cuts are common in early learners. If you have concerns about your child's development, ask your child's teacher or pediatrician.

## A five-day Christmas activity plan

About 15 minutes a day is plenty. Here's one week that touches every skill:

1. **Monday (10 minutes):** Ornament counting tree with the number 5. Count the stickers together twice.
2. **Tuesday (15 minutes):** Trace "From" and your child's name on four gift tags. Tie them onto gifts that evening.
3. **Wednesday (10 minutes):** Fringe snipping with two strips of wrapping paper. Save the scraps for the confetti jar.
4. **Thursday (15 minutes):** Hide the star and tree picture cards. Your child finds each one and traces that word.
5. **Friday (10 minutes):** Bell count and clap, then trace the number for today's date on the countdown sheet.

Next week, raise the ornament number, add two new words, and try four more gift tags. By Christmas, your child will have counted, cut, and signed their way through the season.

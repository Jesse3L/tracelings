The best Christmas crafts for preschoolers use big pieces, a few simple steps, and give kids a job they can mostly handle on their own. Six that work well are a traced name ornament, a signed handprint card, a paper chain countdown, a torn paper tree, a name stocking, and a snowman with a name scarf. Each one includes a step where your child traces or writes their name, so a little handwriting practice comes along with the glue.

Below you'll find one shared supply list, the steps for each craft, and how to adjust them for a 3 year old versus a 5 year old.

## Supplies for these Christmas crafts for preschoolers

Gather this once and keep it in a bin through December:

- Red, green, white, and brown construction paper
- White cardstock and a few paper plates
- Washable paint in red, green, and brown
- Glue sticks and a small bottle of white glue
- Child safety scissors and a hole punch
- Chunky crayons, washable markers, and a highlighter
- Ribbon or yarn
- Cotton balls and a few stickers
- A printed name tracing sheet or two

Skip glitter and small beads if you have babies or toddlers at home, and keep cotton balls and stickers out of their reach. Sit with your preschooler during anything that uses scissors or glue.

## 1. The traced name ornament

This is the craft kids are proudest of, because their name ends up on the tree.

1. Trace around a mug or small bowl on white cardstock and cut out a circle about 3 inches across.
2. Print a [free name tracing worksheet](/name-tracing/) and cut out one line of your child's name. Glue it across the middle of the circle.
3. Your child traces their name with a red or green marker.
4. They decorate the edges with crayon dots, stripes, or stickers.
5. Punch a hole at the top and tie on a loop of ribbon.

Write the year on the back so you can compare names in future Decembers.

**For 3 year olds:** Trace only the first letter, and let them color the rest of the circle freely.

**For 5 year olds:** Have them trace the name on the front, then write it again on the back without dots.

## 2. A signed handprint card

A handprint card is a classic for a reason. Little hands grow fast, and grandparents tend to keep these.

1. Fold a sheet of cardstock in half.
2. Paint your child's palm and fingers green.
3. Press the hand onto the front of the card with the fingers pointing down. Turned this way, it looks like a small tree.
4. When it's dry, your child adds dots of color with a marker for ornaments.
5. Inside, write "Love," and then your child's name in dotted letters.
6. Your child traces the name to sign the card.

Signing a card gives name writing a real purpose, and kids often take more care when a real person will see it.

## 3. A paper chain countdown

A paper chain turns the wait for Christmas into a daily routine, with one link coming off each day.

1. Cut strips of red and green paper, about 1 inch wide and 8 inches long.
2. Make one strip for each day you want to count down.
3. On the last strip, write your child's name in dotted letters. Your child traces it.
4. Show your child how to loop a strip into a ring and glue the ends. Then they thread the next strip through and glue it.
5. Hang the chain with the name link at the bottom.

Each morning, your child tears off one link. The name link is the last one left on Christmas Eve.

Looping and gluing rings is good hand practice. A long chain can be made over two sittings.

## 4. A torn paper Christmas tree

Tearing paper builds finger strength, and it's very hard to do wrong. That makes it a good craft for a child who gets frustrated with scissors.

1. Draw a large triangle on a sheet of paper.
2. Your child tears green paper into small pieces, about the size of a stamp.
3. They glue the pieces inside the triangle until it's covered.
4. Glue on a brown paper rectangle for the trunk.
5. Cut a yellow star, write your child's name on it in dotted letters, and let them trace it.
6. Glue the name star to the top of the tree.

**For 3 year olds:** Tear a few strips ahead of time so they only have to tear them into smaller pieces.

**For 5 year olds:** Let them cut the star themselves and add tiny paper ornaments with their initials.

## 5. A name stocking for the door

This one uses big, bubbly letters, so it's a nice break for a child who is tired of pencils.

1. Print your child's name from our [name coloring pages](/name-coloring-pages/).
2. Draw a large stocking shape on red paper, and cut it out together.
3. Your child colors the bubble letters of their name.
4. Trim around the name and glue it across the top of the stocking.
5. Add a strip of cotton balls along the cuff.
6. Tape the stocking to a bedroom door.

If you have several kids, make one stocking each. It helps them practice spotting their own name among their siblings' names.

## 6. A snowman with a name scarf

This snowman works well for families and classrooms that want a winter craft without a Christmas theme.

1. Glue three white paper circles in a stack on blue paper.
2. Your child glues cotton balls onto the circles.
3. Draw eyes and buttons with a marker, or use small black paper dots.
4. Cut a long strip of red paper for the scarf. Write your child's name on it in dotted letters.
5. Your child traces the name, then glues the scarf across the snowman's neck.

## What to expect at 3, 4 and 5

Children grow at their own pace, so treat these as loose ranges, not targets:

- **Many 3 year olds** are still learning to hold a crayon and make lines and circles. Recognizing their name or tracing one letter is a great goal.
- **Many 4 year olds** can trace their whole name, and some write a few letters on their own. Letters may be large, backward, or out of order.
- **Many 5 year olds** can write their first name without a model and enjoy tracing short words like "Love" or "Merry Christmas."

Backward letters and wobbly lines are very common in early writers. If you have concerns about your child's grip, hand control, or writing, ask your child's teacher or pediatrician.

## Tips for calm crafting in a busy month

- **Prep the hard parts.** Cut circles and strips and write the dotted names before you call your child over.
- **Trace before paint.** Do the name step first, while hands are clean and attention is fresh.
- **Use short, thick tools.** Chunky crayons and markers are easier for small hands to control.
- **Keep sessions short.** Ten to fifteen minutes is plenty. Any craft here can be finished the next day.
- **Have a quiet backup.** A stack of [Christmas coloring pages](/coloring-pages/christmas-coloring-pages/) is perfect for days when energy is low or a craft is drying.

## A two-week Christmas craft plan

About 15 minutes a day, a few days a week, is enough to finish all six:

1. **Day 1:** Make the paper chain. Trace the name link and loop about 10 rings.
2. **Day 2:** Finish the chain and hang it up. Tear off the first link.
3. **Day 3:** Make the torn paper tree, then trace the name star and add it.
4. **Day 4:** Paint the handprint card and let it dry overnight.
5. **Day 5:** Add the ornament dots and sign the card inside. Mail it or set it aside to deliver.
6. **Day 6:** Make the traced name ornament and hang it on the tree.
7. **Day 7:** Color the name stocking and tape it to the bedroom door.
8. **Day 8:** Build the snowman and trace the name scarf.

By the end, your child will have traced their name six times, and every one of those names is hanging somewhere they can see it.

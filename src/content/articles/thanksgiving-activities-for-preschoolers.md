The best Thanksgiving activities for preschoolers are short, low-prep, and quietly build a skill: hand strength, name writing, or counting. Think clipping clothespin feathers onto a paper turkey, tracing a "thankful for" page with their name on it, or counting pie slices at the table. Each one takes 10 to 15 minutes and uses things you probably already have.

Below, the activities are grouped by the skill they build, so you can pick what your child needs most. There's a simple week plan at the end.

## How to pick Thanksgiving activities for preschoolers

Start with what your child is working on right now. A child who still grips a crayon in a fist will get more from pinching and tearing than from tracing letters. A child who can trace their name may be ready to count and write a few numbers.

You don't need to do everything on this list. Two or three activities a week is plenty for most preschoolers. If one falls flat, put it away and try it again in a few days.

## Fine motor activities for little hands

Fine motor skills are the small movements of the fingers and hands. They are what kids use to hold a crayon, snip with scissors, and later form letters. These Thanksgiving activities give those muscles a workout without any writing at all.

- **Turkey feather pinch.** Draw a large turkey body on paper and set out a bowl of clothespins. Your child clips the pins around the edge as feathers. Squeezing a clothespin builds the same pinch used to hold a pencil.
- **Corn kernel transfer.** Pour dried corn kernels or dried beans into one bowl. Your child moves them to another bowl one at a time with fingers or kid tweezers. Sit close by, since small items are a choking risk.
- **Torn paper leaves.** Draw leaf outlines on a sheet of paper. Your child tears red, orange, and yellow paper into small pieces and glues them inside the lines. Tearing uses both hands together, and the gluing builds control.
- **Playdough pies.** Roll out playdough, press it into a small foil tin, and cut "slices" with a plastic knife. Rolling and pressing are great for hand strength.
- **Leaf rubbings.** Lay a real leaf under thin paper and rub over it with the side of a peeled crayon. Holding a short crayon on its side encourages a finger grip instead of a fist.

## Name writing activities with a Thanksgiving twist

A name is often the first word a preschooler wants to write. These activities give it a reason to show up on paper.

- **Thankful name page.** This is the centerpiece activity, with full steps below.
- **Gratitude leaves.** Cut out paper leaves. Write your child's name in dotted lines on each one, and they trace it. Then they tell you one thing they're thankful for and you write it underneath. Tape the leaves to a window or a paper tree trunk.
- **Name on the napkin rings.** Cut a cardboard tube into rings. Your child traces their name, or just the first letter, on a strip of paper and wraps it around a ring. Make one for each family member if they're keen.
- **Name letter hunt.** Write the letters of your child's name on sticky notes and hide them around one room. Once found, they line them up in order on the table.

For the tracing parts, a printed [free name tracing worksheet](/name-tracing/) gives your child clean, evenly sized letters. You can cut out one line and glue it onto a leaf or a ring.

## How to make a thankful name page

1. Print one line of your child's name from the name tracing page and glue it near the top of a sheet of paper.
2. Under it, write "I am thankful for" in dotted letters.
3. Your child traces their name first, then the words, if they have the energy.
4. They draw or glue pictures of what they're thankful for in the space below: family, a pet, a favorite food.
5. Write a label for each drawing as they tell you about it.

For a 3 year old, tracing the first letter of their name and drawing one picture is a full, worthwhile session. Many 4 and 5 year olds can trace the whole name and some of the words.

## Counting activities for Thanksgiving week

Counting fits naturally into a holiday that's all about food and people. These keep numbers playful.

- **Count the guests.** Help your child count how many people are coming, then count out that many napkins, spoons, or cups.
- **Feathers on a turkey.** Roll a die and add that many paper feathers or clothespins to the turkey from the fine motor section. Count them together as they go on.
- **Pie slice math.** Draw a circle "pie" and cut it into 4 or 6 slices. Count the slices, take some away, and count what's left.
- **Number tracing on placemats.** On a paper placemat, dot out the numbers 1 to 5 in a corner. Your child traces them and draws that many peas, cranberries, or leaves next to each one.

If your child is ready for more number practice, the [number tracing worksheets for 1 to 10](/number-tracing/1-10/) pair well with any of these. Trace a number, then count out that many kernels or feathers.

## A coloring station for the long wait

Thanksgiving Day often includes a long stretch while the food cooks. A small coloring station on a side table keeps preschoolers busy and gives cousins something to share.

Set out crayons and a stack of [Thanksgiving coloring pages](/coloring-pages/thanksgiving-coloring-pages/). Coloring inside larger shapes is also quiet fine motor practice. Older cousins can help younger ones, and the finished pages make an easy wall display.

## What to expect from preschoolers at this age

Preschoolers vary a lot, so treat these as loose ranges:

- **Many 3 year olds** are still building hand strength and making lines and circles. Recognizing their name or tracing one letter is a great goal.
- **Many 4 year olds** can trace their whole name and count small groups of objects, often up to 10.
- **Many 5 year olds** write their first name on their own and can count and trace numbers to 10 or beyond.

Wobbly lines, backward letters, and skipped numbers are common in early learners. If you have concerns about your child's grip, hand control, or counting, ask your child's teacher or pediatrician. For grip questions, an occupational therapist can also help.

## Tips for keeping it fun

- **Prep before you call them over.** Draw the turkey, dot out the name, and set out materials first.
- **Stop while it's going well.** Ten to fifteen minutes is enough. Ending on a good note makes the next session easier.
- **Use real jobs.** Counting napkins for the actual table feels more important than counting on a worksheet.
- **Name what they did.** "You pinched all eight clothespins" means more than "good job."

## A simple Thanksgiving week of activities

Here's one way to fit these into the week before Thanksgiving, about 15 minutes a day:

1. **Monday:** Turkey feather pinch with clothespins. Roll a die and count the feathers as they go on.
2. **Tuesday:** Torn paper leaves, then trace a name on two or three gratitude leaves.
3. **Wednesday:** Make the thankful name page. Trace the name, then draw what they're thankful for.
4. **Thursday:** Corn kernel transfer, then trace the numbers 1 to 5 and count kernels for each one.
5. **Friday:** Playdough pies. Cut slices, count them, and take some away.
6. **Thanksgiving Day:** Count the guests, set out that many napkins, and open the coloring station while dinner cooks.

Keep the thankful name page somewhere you'll find it next year. Seeing how much a name changes from one November to the next is a sweet surprise for the whole family.

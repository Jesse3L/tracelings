The best Thanksgiving crafts for preschoolers are simple, a little messy, and have a real job to do, like decorating the table or greeting guests. Three that work well are a handprint turkey with a traced name, a feather name banner, and a place card your child makes for each person at dinner. Each one gets kids writing or tracing their own name without it feeling like a worksheet.

Below you'll find supplies, steps, and notes on how to adjust each craft for a 3 year old versus a 5 year old.

## Why name crafts work so well in November

A name is usually the first word a preschooler cares about writing. Thanksgiving gives that word a reason to exist. A name on a turkey gets hung on the fridge. A name on a place card tells Grandpa where to sit.

That real purpose is what makes the practice stick. Kids will often trace a name carefully on a craft that they would scribble through on a plain sheet of paper. You also end up with decorations, which is a nice bonus in a busy week.

## The supply list for all three crafts

One basic kit covers all three crafts:

- Brown, orange, red, and yellow construction paper
- Washable paint in brown, orange, red, and yellow
- Glue sticks and a small bottle of white glue
- Child safety scissors
- Crayons, washable markers, and a highlighter
- Craft feathers or paper feathers you cut ahead of time
- Yarn or string and a hole punch
- Index cards or cardstock folded in half
- A printed name tracing sheet or two

Keep small items like craft feathers, googly eyes, and pom poms away from babies and toddlers in the house. Sit with your preschooler during anything with scissors or glue.

## Handprint turkey with a traced name

This is the classic, with one change: the name goes on the turkey's body.

1. Paint your child's palm brown and their fingers in orange, red, and yellow.
2. Press the hand flat onto a sheet of paper. The thumb becomes the head, and the fingers are the feathers.
3. Let it dry for about 20 minutes.
4. Write your child's name across the palm print in large dotted letters with a pencil.
5. Your child traces the dots with a marker.
6. Add an eye, a beak, and legs with a marker.

For a smoother result, print a [free name tracing worksheet](/name-tracing/), cut out one line of your child's name, and glue it under the turkey. Then your child traces a clean, evenly spaced name instead of your quick dots.

**For 3 year olds:** Do the painting together and trace the first letter only. Say the letter as they trace.

**For 5 year olds:** Have them trace the whole name, then write it again on their own under the turkey. Many 5 year olds also enjoy tracing "Happy Thanksgiving" if you dot it out first.

## Feather name banner

A banner spreads the name out so each letter gets its own space. That makes it easier for little hands than squeezing letters onto one line.

1. Cut one large paper feather for each letter of your child's name. Use a mix of fall colors.
2. Write one letter on each feather in big dotted lines. Use a capital for the first letter and lowercase for the rest.
3. Your child traces each letter with a marker or crayon.
4. Let them decorate the feathers with crayon stripes, stickers, or glued craft feathers.
5. Punch a hole at the top of each feather and string them in order on yarn.
6. Hang it on a door, a window, or the back of a chair.

Putting the letters in order is part of the activity. Lay the feathers out in a mixed-up pile and work together to spell the name. Point to each one and say the letter as it goes on the string.

**For 3 year olds:** Trace just the first letter yourself as a model, then let them color every feather freely. Stringing big feathers on yarn is great hand practice on its own.

**For 5 year olds:** Skip the dotted lines on one or two feathers and let them write those letters freehand.

## Place cards for the family table

This craft gives preschoolers a real role in the holiday. It also gets them practicing other people's names, which they often find exciting.

1. Fold index cards or cardstock in half so they stand up.
2. Write each guest's name in dotted letters on the front of a card.
3. Your child traces the names and adds a drawing, like a leaf, a pie, or a turkey.
4. On Thanksgiving, let them set each card at a seat.

If you have a long guest list, split it up. Your child makes cards for the people closest to them, and you handle the rest. Two or three cards a day across a week keeps it fun.

You can also print [name coloring pages](/name-coloring-pages/) for each guest and fold or trim them into place cards. Your child colors the bubbly letters instead of tracing, which is a nice option for a child who is tired of pencils.

## A coloring corner for the big day

Thanksgiving often means a long wait while dinner cooks. A small coloring station keeps preschoolers busy and gives cousins something to do together.

Set out crayons and a stack of [Thanksgiving coloring pages](/coloring-pages/thanksgiving-coloring-pages/) on a side table. Add a few name coloring pages for the kids who are coming. Kids love finding a page with their own name on it, and older cousins can help younger ones color the letters.

When the pages are done, tape them to a wall or window as a gallery for the family to see.

## What to expect at 3 versus 5

Every child moves at their own pace, so think of these as loose ranges:

- **Many 3 year olds** are still working on holding a crayon and making lines and circles. Tracing one letter, or recognizing their name, is a great goal.
- **Many 4 year olds** can trace their whole name and may write a few letters on their own. Letters may be big, backward, or out of order.
- **Many 5 year olds** can write their first name without a model and enjoy copying other short words.

Backward letters and wobbly lines are very common at this age. If you have concerns about your child's grip, hand control, or writing, ask your child's teacher or pediatrician.

## Tips that keep crafts from turning into tears

- **Prep ahead.** Cut feathers and write dotted names before you call your child over. Waiting is hard for preschoolers.
- **Use thick tools.** Chunky markers and short crayons are easier to control than thin pencils.
- **Paint last.** Do the tracing before the paint comes out, while hands are still clean and focused.
- **Keep it short.** Ten to fifteen minutes is plenty. You can finish a craft over two days.
- **Praise the effort.** Point out a letter that started at the top or a line that stayed on the dots.

## A simple Thanksgiving week plan

Spread the crafts across the week before Thanksgiving, about 15 minutes a day:

1. **Monday:** Paint the handprint turkey and let it dry overnight.
2. **Tuesday:** Trace the name on the turkey and add the face and legs. Hang it on the fridge.
3. **Wednesday:** Trace and decorate half the feathers for the name banner.
4. **Thursday:** Finish the feathers, put the letters in order, and string the banner.
5. **Friday:** Make two or three place cards for the closest family members.
6. **Weekend:** Finish the place cards and set up the coloring corner supplies in a bin.

On Thanksgiving Day, hand your child the place cards and let them set the table. It's a small job, and it's their name practice put to real use.
